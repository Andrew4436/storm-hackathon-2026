# Evaluation: neighbourhood monthly forecast, horizon 2 months

Generated by `ml/src/evaluate.py` from `data/processed/neighbourhood_monthly.csv` (data through 2026-08;
2026-10 is forecast from it). All numbers are reported incidents per neighbourhood per month
or their severity-weighted index; nothing here is a rating of any place.

## Decision

- Rule (fixed before the run, `config.DECISION_METRIC = "mae_pooled_weighted_index"`): ship whichever of
  Poisson GLM and Random Forest has the lower pooled MAE on `weighted_index`.
- GLM variant: `poisson_glm` (pooled weighted_index MAE 559.9 without the
  area one-hot vs 606.0 with it; lower wins).
- **Shipped model: `poisson_glm`** (pooled weighted_index MAE: GLM 559.9, Random Forest 611.6).
- 12-month mean, for reference: 585.1. The lead over the 12-month mean is **small**: 4.3% lower pooled MAE on weighted_index, 3.2% on incident_count. A different test year could reverse it; the honest summary is that the GLM is about as accurate as a 12-month average, slightly better overall. **In fold B (2026-01..2026-08) the 12-month mean was more accurate** (weighted_index MAE 494.8 vs GLM 528.7), so the pooled lead comes from the other fold.
- GLM beats the 12-month mean on weighted_index in each fold? fold A: yes, fold B: no. It beats it in 14 of 24 areas (pooled).
- Blend (0.5 x GLM + 0.5 x 12-month mean), reported only: pooled weighted_index MAE 562.5.

## Setup

- Target month T = origin month t + 2. Features use months up to t only; 2026-09 (partial) is never used.
- Expanding window, split by target month; training targets start at 2010-01.
- Every method is scored on identical test rows. "Pooled" = folds A and B together (480 rows per target).

| fold | train target months | train rows | test target months | test rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 2010-01..2024-12 | 4320 | 2025-01..2025-12 | 288 |
| B | 2010-01..2025-12 | 4608 | 2026-01..2026-08 | 192 |

Metrics: MAE and RMSE in the target's units; WAPE = sum |error| / sum actual; MAE excl. CBD drops
Central Business District (roughly 4x any other area); "areas beating mean_12" counts the 24 areas whose
own MAE is below the 12-month mean's.

## Tier metrics (pooled)

The predicted tier applies the tier rule to the forecast; the realised tier applies it to the actual value.
Both are measured against the same "typical" level: the area's mean over the 36 complete
months ending at the origin month. Thresholds (-5.0, 5.0) %; areas averaging under
10 incidents/month are `insufficient_data` and excluded here.

Realised tiers in the pooled test set (how often each tier actually happened):

| target | below_typical | typical | above_typical | insufficient_data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| weighted_index | 281 | 79 | 100 | 20 |
| incident_count | 231 | 80 | 149 | 20 |

Read these with care: 61.1% of realised weighted_index tiers in the test folds (2025-01..2025-12 and 2026-01..2026-08) are `below_typical`
(measured against each area's own 36-month mean), so a forecast that mostly says that tier scores well.
Always predicting `below_typical` would score 61.1% accuracy, higher than every method here (best: blend at 53.9%), so tier accuracy should not be presented as skill. The shipped GLM's tier accuracy of
53% with macro-F1 0.38 means the predicted tier is a weak signal at the current thresholds;
with forecast errors of about 13% (GLM WAPE above), a -5%..+5% band is narrower than the forecast error. The thresholds are the team's call at the 7 PM checkpoint.

## Caveats

- Two test periods (2025-01..2025-12 and 2026-01..2026-08) are a small sample; differences of a few percent in MAE between
  methods are not reliable.
- Recent months of Offence Against a Person are under-reported (reporting lag), which can pull fold B actuals down.
- Category mix shifts over time (Theft from Vehicle down, Other Theft up), so the weighted index and the count can move differently.
